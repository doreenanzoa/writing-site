Nyara (His Order Her Oath)

My debut novel. Published by Desha Publishing House.

She was raised to obey. He was trained to hunt. Nyara is a weapon built by a man who taught her that loyalty was love. Elias is the hunter determined to dismantle the empire she calls home. But when buried truths surface and loyalties fracture, their worlds collide. He's after the truth. She's after revenge. And somewhere between his order and her oath, everything changes.

In the distance, laughter echoes, slurred, unhinged, high pitched. A motorcycle roars to life, could be stolen, wouldn't be a first. The city never sleeps, always alive in one way or another. During the day, it's noisy, crowded, chaos, traffic and people. Too many people. In the night, it's less chaotic, still noisy but the kind you can ignore. Drunks, thieves, bars, clubs, churches hosting night prayers, witches and wizards praying to who knows what. Ignorable. Me? I work. I sit lazily on the ledge of a rooftop, watching an empty office three floors below like it's the most interesting thing in the world. Waiting. The bag of chips in my hand empties too fast for my liking, but patience has never been my weakness. 10:50, 10:55,11:00. Just on time. The door practically flies open, my target walks in. Honourable John Micho Alfred, big belly, double chin, the kind of smile that makes children cry, a height too short to keep up with his weight. He flops on his seat with the same confidence that bleeds out of him on television. The kind that convinces him he's unstoppable, untouchable, that he owns the town and can do as he pleases. The kind that gives him the courage to run his mouth without thinking twice, to bite the hand that feeds him and expect to survive. He's caught Victor's attention. When you get Victor's attention, you get mine. Bad thing. He's on the phone, talking, laughing. I can almost imagine the sound of it, he's gesturing animatedly, clearly drunk on whatever praise he's getting on the other side. The empty bag of chips drops down and I slide off the ledge. The rifle comes together beneath my hands like muscle memory, familiar, alive. Once in position, I watch him through the scope, having his last conversation. At least he'll die doing what he does best, talking. My breathing slows, the world fades into the background and my body goes still in a way that only ever promises blood. Do the job. Clean. No hesitation. Victor's voice echoes in my head as I adjust my stance and align the shot. My finger hovers just above the trigger. Then, something changes. Through the scope, I see his laughter fade, the door creaks open, someone else enters the room, and Micho sets his phone down. The air shifts, I can almost taste the change in the atmosphere. A foreigner. Interesting. Not staff, not security. A man, tall, controlled. He's got different posture, confidence and the kind that doesn't need noise. He slides something across the table, a file. He's not speaking, doesn't even sit, just watches and it's clear who's in control. I exhale through my nose. Small inconveniences I have no time for. I raise back my gun and change the angle. This time, I take my shot, clean, quiet and precise. It breaks the glass with a dull crack. The honourable drops face first onto the table, a pool of crimson spreads beneath him, soaking files and papers, blood replaces ink. I should leave, the job's done. But the reaction of the foreigner keeps me rooted, or the lack thereof. He doesn't flinch, doesn't shift, just watches the body like it's a boring article in a newspaper, his posture doesn't even change. He's not just watching, he's assessing, observing. Then slowly, with a chilling calm. He looks up, in my exact direction. He can't see me, it's dark here and I can tell by the way he's looking, there's no clear focus but something in my stomach knots anyway. His face even from afar is striking, sharp features that could've only been crafted in hell. Intense eyes that are vividly blue and unsettling, like he could see right through the darkness, through the rifle by sheer will. The door opens again, a lady walks in and she screams, he looks away, he's distracted and that's enough. I quickly dismantle my rifle and put her back in the bag. I don't stick around for the dramatics and leave down the stairwell. In unhurried movements. The job's done, it's clean. But I can't shake off that piercing blue blank stare, even as I reach the ground floor.

What my stories are about

Love that arrives uninvited

My characters don't choose who they fall for. Love happens to them, and then they have to live with what it asks of them.

Darkness that costs something

No dark choice is free in my books. Every one leaves a mark on the people who made it.

Worlds that feel like home

My settings are rooted in recognisable African social worlds, where family and community can hold you up or hold you back.

About the writer

I'm a Ugandan writer based in Kampala. My storytelling started in secondary school, where I narrated invented films to my dormmates, and I've been chasing that feeling on the page ever since.

When I'm not writing, I'm building websites as a software engineering student. You can see that side of me in my developer portfolio.
